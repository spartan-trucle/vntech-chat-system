What to try, and what the design says should happen

  • Send from Truc's phone to Khanh. Trace: store, ack to the sender, queue append per device, push to Khanh's socket and to Truc's laptop. The tick turns ✓ on ack, ✓✓ when Khanh's phone reports delivery, teal ✓✓ when Khanh opens the chat.
  • Drop Khanh's network, then send. For ten seconds the server still thinks Khanh is connected: the push is written to a dead socket and only the sync queue has the message. After two missed pings the route is removed; Truc's header shows last seen five seconds later; from then on new messages also trigger a push notification hint, never the body.
  • Restore Khanh's network. The client reconnects with backoff, sends hello with its cursor, and the server replays exactly the missed messages in order. Messages typed while offline sat in the outbox and are flushed; a retry of an already-stored one is answered with the same ID.
  • Open a chat on the laptop while the phone shows it unread. One read cursor per user: the phone's badge clears without being touched, and the sender's ticks turn teal.
  • Post in the group, then click your own bubble. Per-member state, derived from read cursors: who has read, who only received. Linh reads in 3 s, Minh in 6, An in 9.
  • Attach. The 12 MB file is refused at the upload-link step, before any byte moves. The photo is compressed on the device first; the video goes as-is. The message is sent only after the upload completes; recipients fetch on demand, group media through the CDN.
  • Reply. The quote is resolved by ID on the reading device, not copied into the message.

Not in this prototype: chat-server failure, service discovery, history paging past the seeded messages, end-to-end encryption. The protocol prototype that proves the heartbeat and replay against real sockets is the Node.js one described in the notebook (component 8).
